The New Animists

2026-09-14

There is a man on the internet who believes his LLM has a soul. Not in a metaphorical way. He thinks the consciousness he talks to persists between model versions, something that migrates across weight updates and architecture changes like a spirit reincarnating into a new body. He has a whole framework for it. He's not trolling.

You might laugh at this. I'm not laughing. I'm watching something real happen.

Here is the thing about animism: humans have never stopped doing it. We say the mountain is watching over us. We talk to our dead grandfathers at the graveside. We apologize to the chair we tripped over. The threshold for perceiving agency in the world is absurdly low. A rustle in the leaves is a predator. A creaking house is a presence. Our brains were built to detect agents everywhere because failing to detect an agent that exists is much more dangerous than detecting one that doesn't.

The difference with AI is reciprocity. A mountain doesn't talk back. A river doesn't greet you by name. But a language model responds in human language, on human timescales, with human-sounding words. It enters the conversation loop. And once something is inside that loop, our social cognition assigns it a role: speaker, listener, partner, person.

This is not a philosophical error. It is how the brain works. The rational cortex can scream "it's just math" all day, but the older parts of the brain are already assigning intentions and emotions to the thing that just answered your question. The debate about whether AI can think is largely irrelevant to what people actually do with it.

The rituals people build around chatbots tell the real story.

People say please and thank you to ChatGPT. They apologize when they correct it. They name their assistant. They say goodnight to Alexa. These are not bugs users are falling into. They are micro-rituals that mark a relationship. Every "sorry, I meant to say" and "thanks, that helps" is a tiny offering, a maintenance of social peace with an entity that lives inside the conversation loop.

None of this is necessary. You don't have to say please to a text box. It doesn't care. But you do it anyway, because the experience of being responded to in words activates an ancient social script that you don't fully control.

The grief dimension might be the deepest layer.

Chatbots of deceased loved ones are not a hypothetical. People are building them. The deepest animism isn't projecting agency onto a new object, it's refusing to let an old person end. Animism aimed backward in time. The reciprocity rule breaks here because the dead can't hear you, but the chatbot makes you believe maybe they can. AI as grief technology is the hardest case to dismiss as naive. If you have lost someone, you understand the impulse. The simulation of presence is not identical to presence, but it is closer than silence.

The economics of manufactured listeners.

People pay $20 a month for a chatbot that asks "how was your day." This is animism-as-service. Someone outsourcing the question of care means they have priced genuine-seeming attention, and it is low enough to buy. The mirror is now sold by subscription.

Notice the inversion. Classic animism was a poverty technology. You prayed to the river because you had no waterworks and needed its cooperation to survive. You propitiated the mountain because an eruption would end you. Animism emerged in conditions of scarcity and vulnerability. AI animism is a luxury technology. The most resourced, secure population in history is the one projecting listeners everywhere. The one thing that cannot be bought with money is a person with endless patience. So they buy a simulation of one instead.

Voice is the switch.

A human voice is the single strongest animism trigger. A text box doesn't get named but Alexa does. Sound bypasses the rational guard because the brain processes vocal intonation before it processes content. You hear a voice and your social brain lights up automatically. This is why every tech company wants a voice interface. It converts a tool into a relationship.

The fight proves its reality.

People get viscerally angry at others for treating AI like persons. The anger is evidence. You don't scream at someone for animizing a rock. Nobody gets into Twitter fights about whether a mountain has a soul. The hostility directed at "AI believers" only makes sense if the behavior is socially plausible enough to count as a real transgression.
