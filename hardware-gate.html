The Hardware Gate

2026-10-05

Hardware attestation is quietly becoming the internet's identity layer. Banking apps check Play Integrity before they open. Government identity wallets require a secure enclave. Websites fingerprint your GPU through WebGL. Email providers scan QR codes from your phone. None of these systems were designed together, but they add up to the same thing: verification is shifting from what you know (passwords) to what you have (hardware) to who vouches for you (corporate attestation). Each step centralizes trust in a smaller set of gatekeepers.

There are two kinds of attestation. The visible kind hits you with an error message: your banking app refuses to open because a Play Integrity check failed. You see the block, you know why. The invisible kind is browser fingerprinting - WebGL canvas rendering, GPU model detection, Cloudflare Turnstile analyzing your audio context. You don't see it happening. You just notice that some websites feel harder to access, or that you keep getting challenged with QR code scans while other people breeze through. The invisible kind is worse because you can't fight what you can't see.

Why is this happening? Account takeover fraud is expensive. Chargebacks cost platforms money. Deepfakes make identity verification harder every quarter. The rational response for any platform is to shift the verification burden onto hardware that provides cryptographic proof of its own integrity. The problem is that this creates a world where your ability to participate online depends on whose hardware you carry and whether their attestation services approve of you.

The endgame is a split internet. One layer for attested devices where you can access banking, government services, and premium content. Another layer for everything else - slower, more suspicious, constantly challenging you. The device in your pocket determines which internet you live in, and you don't get a vote.
